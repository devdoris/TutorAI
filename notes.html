Tutor: prototype and roadmap

What the prototype shows, how it maps to the PRD, what is not built yet, and where AI comes in next.

1. What it is The two layers 2. Demo script 3. PRD fit 4. Not built yet 5. Where AI comes in 6. Next steps

1. What this prototype is

A working prototype of the core teaching loop. A stick-figure tutor teaches on a chalkboard: it speaks, points at what it is talking about, asks the learner to tap, type or drag an answer, answers questions mid-lesson, and records progress by what the learner did without help.

Underneath, it is now two layers that do not know about each other: one writes scripts, the other plays them. The board will perform any script it is handed, whoever wrote it and whenever it turns up.

"The teaching experience is proved, and the execution engine is now separate from whatever writes the lesson. Plugging in a generator changes nothing on this side."

The two layers

One layer writes the script. The other performs it. The performing side has no idea what a curriculum is, where a script came from, or whether a person or a model wrote it. Hand it a script and it plays it — that is the whole contract, and a test fails if the engine ever reaches back into the content.
A teacher, a file, a service, a model→ a script→ the engine plays it

What a script looks like

Every step is one thing the tutor does. at is what it points at; x and y are percentages of the board, which is how the tutor knows where to aim its arm.

DirectiveWhat it does
saySpeaks. Nothing on the board changes
boardSets the heading, subheading or kind of board
show / hide / clearPuts things on the board and takes them off
point / trace / compareMoves the tutor's arm and speaks
askStops and waits: tap, type or drag
praise / listen / waitCelebrates, invites a question, holds still

How a script gets in

  • The Script intake panel on the Board: paste one, or upload a .json file
  • Drop a .json file anywhere on the page
  • From another window: win.postMessage({ type: "tutor:script", script }, "*")
  • From the console: TutorEngine.run(script)
  • From a service: open the app with ?script=https://…/lesson.json

Whichever route it takes, it is checked against the format first, and the tag beside the lesson title says which layer produced what is playing: library, file, api or generator.

Files

2. Demo script (about 8 minutes)

  1. Lessons screen. Nine objectives across Maths, English, Science, Geography and Music. Switch the curriculum (Nigeria / England / United States). "Curriculum is a setting, not the product. The experience stays the same."
  2. Open "Reading a big number out loud" and let it run. It writes the number one digit at a time, and the tutor's arm moves to whatever it is naming. "Nothing is drawn on that board without being pointed at."
  3. Press Pause mid-sentence. The button now reads Resume from step N. Press it: the tutor repeats that step rather than losing it.
  4. The comma. Skip ahead with Next step to where the tutor counts three digits from the right, draws the comma, and explains what it is for. "Count three from the right, put the comma there, and it tells you where the thousands end."
  5. Press "Ask the tutor" and type "why is there a zero?" "That question was just turned into a script. The same engine is playing it, on the same board, and the lesson is holding its place." Press Back to step N: the lesson picks up exactly where it stopped. Then ask "who won the football?" (use exactly this question). "It stays in scope. It won't guess; it flags the question for the teacher."
  6. Press "Explain it simpler." The same idea drops to apples and bags of ten. Back to step N, then Go deeper for powers of ten. "One concept, three depths, one engine."
  7. Any script, any time. Open Script intake, paste a short script and press Run it — or drop a .json file onto the window. "Whatever writes the script — a teacher, a service, a model — this side does not change."
  8. Open "Parts of a plant cell", drag the labels onto the diagram, then Check my answer. "Same tutor and loop, different subject, a different kind of interaction — and an old lesson file the engine plays unchanged."
  9. Progress screen. "We measure mastery, not completion. Answers with and without help are counted separately."

Before presenting: on Progress, press Clear this learner's record. Open the app with start.command, then on the Board press Test voice to check the sound. Chrome or Edge gives the best voice.

4. What is not built yet

  1. Pilot depth. One lesson is built out properly (Reading a big number, 56 steps, three levels). The rest are still the original six-step outlines, and the pilot needs millions and billions with practice that moves from guided to independent.
  2. Curriculum adaptation is shallow. Switching changes grade names and which lessons are available, not terminology ("units" vs "ones") or sequencing.
  3. Mastery covers 2 of 5 measures. Accuracy and independence are measured; retention, transfer and misconception recovery are not.
  4. Learner view only. No parent, teacher, school or reviewer views yet.
  5. No model yet. Lessons are authored scripts, and the answer generator is deterministic: it recognises the questions this lesson provokes and refuses the rest rather than inventing. The seam is built — setProvider() hands generation to a model or a service without the engine changing — but nothing is wired to one.
  6. Accessibility. The chalkboard is drawn on a canvas, which screen readers cannot read and the keyboard cannot operate.
  7. Progress is saved in the browser only. Nothing syncs to a server, teacher or other device.

5. Where AI comes in

AI writes the script, the board performs it. That split is no longer a plan — it is how the app is built. The board, the tutor's gestures and the progress tracking are predictable code that plays scripts. A model produces the same scripts a teacher would, arrives through the same checked intake, and is played the same way. It never draws on the board directly and never chats freely with a child, which keeps it safe, reviewable and inside the curriculum.

The plug is one line: setProvider(remoteProvider("https://…/generate")). A provider returns a script, or nothing, in which case the built-in generator answers. Neither the engine nor the board is told.

Lesson creation (offline)

Curriculum objective→ AI drafts lesson data→ Human reviewer approves→ Lesson library

During a lesson (live)

Learner question or answer→ AI, limited to this lesson→ App checks the reply→ Tutor says it and points

Where AI plugs in, in order of value

WhereWhat the AI doesPRD link
1Ask the tutor liveAnswers the learner's own questions, limited to the current lesson. Returns the answer, what to point at on the board, and whether to flag it for the teacherQuestion interruption; child safety
2Marking answers liveAccepts answers the keyword check misses ("forty thousand"). When wrong, works out why and picks the right re-teachMisconception recovery; dignified feedback
3Writing lessons offlineDrafts a full lesson from a curriculum objective. A human approves it before any child sees itContent pipeline; curriculum integrity
4Curriculum wording offlineRewrites lessons in local terms ("units" vs "ones", "JSS1" vs "Year 7"). Also reviewedLocalization; curriculum as a variable
5Fresh practice liveGenerates new practice questions for retention and transfer. For maths, code double-checks every answerRetention and transfer
6ReportsWrites plain-language progress summaries for parents and teachersParents, teachers, schools

Items 1 and 2 make the tutor feel intelligent to a learner. Items 3 and 4 let it scale to any country and curriculum, which is the PRD's central claim.

How it would be built

  • A small server sits between the app and the AI model, so the API key never reaches the browser. One endpoint per feature, e.g. /ask, /mark, /draft-lesson.
  • Models: a fast model (e.g. Claude Haiku 4.5) for live features, so a child never waits; a stronger model (e.g. Claude Sonnet 5) for writing and adapting lessons offline.
  • Safety: live replies come back in a fixed format the app checks. Anything off-topic, malformed or unsafe falls back to "I'll flag that for your teacher." Nothing AI-written reaches a lesson without human review.

How this answers the PRD's open questions

PRD open questionOur answer
Is the board hand-authored or generated?Both. The board performs lesson data; AI drafts that data and humans approve it
How is content produced at scale?AI drafts per curriculum objective and reviewers approve, instead of writing from scratch
How is the conversation kept in scope?The AI sees only the current lesson, replies in a checked format, and falls back to flagging the teacher
How is curriculum context applied?Curriculum is a setting. AI adapts wording per curriculum, reviewed once and reused

6. Suggested next steps

  1. Add AI to Ask the tutor: the biggest live impact for the smallest build.
  2. Build out the pilot lesson: Large Whole Numbers to millions and billions, with more practice.
  3. Add curriculum terminology switching, drafted by AI and reviewed by a human.
  4. Add retention checks (revisit questions days later) and a teacher view.
  5. Add an accessible, keyboard-friendly layer over the board.
